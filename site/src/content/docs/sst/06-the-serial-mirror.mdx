> *In this page:* how the booted sample’s serial reaches the twin’s
> identification block, why one boot is one sample, and the custody
> discipline the platform’s Connect proof depends on.

---

## 1. The twin is the sampled unit

An SST boots against one sample — the named starting condition with
its own chain of custody — and the twin the session serves is that
unit, not the type in general. The runtime makes the identity literal:
at boot, the sample’s declared `serial_number` mirrors into the
instrument model’s identification block before the schema is
generated, so both faces of the twin channel serve it with no schema
edit. The GraphQL query
`{ instrument { identification { serial } } }` and the REST projection
`GET /twin/instrument` answer the same value.

The behavior is pinned by the runtime’s own test: a default boot of
the Steelyard LC-500 answers `LC500-001` (the fresh sample), an explicit
`aged-2024` boot answers `LC500-007`, and the REST projection carries
the identical string, so the two channels cannot drift.

## 2. One boot, one sample

The library’s doctrine is one boot, one sample, one chain of custody.
A physics variant is a boot-time sample, never a runtime switch: the
the Steelyard LC-500’s `creep-fail` boot serves `LC500-002`, and rebooting with
that sample means the sim is now a different instrument. A presenter
or an operator who leaves the previous boot in place while the record
expects the next sample meets the mismatch described below, which is
the custody machinery working, not a fault.

## 3. The custody-identity discipline

The platform’s Connect proof compares the twin’s served serial against
the sample’s custody identity, verbatim. The direction of declaration
matters: the platform’s sample record moves to the serial the twin
serves, never the reverse. The operator declares the twin’s own serial
on the sample record — `LC500-001` for the fresh boot — and Connect
proves that the instrument on the wire is the sample that was shipped.

A generated serial breaks the proof. If the sample’s receipt is
stamped with a serial the platform invented, the twin cannot serve it,
and Connect refuses with the blocker named: a serial mismatch reads as
“the twin serves … but the sample declares …”, which is a custody
failure, not a retry. The refusal moves nothing — the landed run stays
untouched and every field remains enterable by hand — because a
binding that does not name this sample’s twin must never silently
stand in for it.

The same discipline is what makes the failure-branch demonstrations
honest: each rehearsal sample (`creep-fail`, `temp-fail`,
`drift-fail`, `lying-twin`, `stale-twin`) serves its own serial, and
the platform leg that exercises it declares that serial on its own
sample record.
