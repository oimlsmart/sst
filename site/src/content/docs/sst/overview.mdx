> *In this page:* what the Simulated SMART Twin is, the two repositories
> it lives in, and the three things it is for.

---

## 1. What an SST is

A Simulated SMART Twin is a faithful, scriptable simulation of a SMART
Measuring Instrument. Faithful means the twin surface is identical: the
sim answers the same GraphQL a physical SMART instrument answers, with
the same IS/HAS/DOES channels, the same serve-time and freshness rules,
and the same degradation to indeterminate when the value cannot prove
itself. Scriptable means the world behind the twin is driven openly:
loads are placed and removed, the chamber's temperature and humidity
change, time advances in steps.

An SST is plainly marked as simulation. Its ground truth is a declared
probe channel (`sim_ground_truth`), never confused with a physical
reference, and its twin says what it is.

## 2. The two repositories

The SST is split the way the whole SMART program is split: machinery
apart from content.

- **Primmel SST (the framework).** The runtime, the twin and world
  drivers, the console grammar, the shell, the bench. Everything that
  does not care what the instrument measures.
- **OIML SST (the instrument library).** The kinds (the physics of each
  instrument family) and the instances (the named example devices that
  use them): the load cell, the radar speed meter, the dimensional
  instrument, the gas analyzer, and the sampling-line subsystem.

A new instrument kind is a package in the library; the framework does
not change. The library validates and boots against the framework in
CI, so a physics regression in a kind fails the gate, not a user.

## 3. What it is for

- **Training.** A tester rehearses the full certification workflow on
  the sim: prepare the test, drive the chamber and the load through the
  method, record the readings, read the verdicts. The OIML-CS SMART
  platform's guided runs drive the same sims step by step.
- **Integration.** A developer binds a client to the twin endpoint
  without a lab: the SMART platform, a monitoring engine, or their own
  software talks to the sim exactly as it will talk to the physical
  instrument.
- **Honest rehearsal.** The physics is realistic enough to matter:
  creep, temperature drift, per-channel response, and the small
  differences a temperature cycle leaves behind, configurable by the
  user.
