> *In this page:* the twin endpoint’s channels, the world endpoint
> beside it, and how the SMART platform binds an endpoint to perform
> tests.

---

## 1. The twin endpoint

Every booted sim serves `/twin`: the SMART twin surface, as GraphQL.
The channels match the SMART Measuring Instrument’s own:

- **IS**: the instrument’s identity — kind, model, serial, the type it
  claims.
- **HAS**: its registers — the environmental context (temperature,
  humidity, pressure), configuration, declared characteristics.
- **DOES**: its behavior — the indication process with serve time, and
  the declared operations (zero, span, self-test), each with its
  preconditions.

Every served value carries its provenance and serve time; the same
freshness rules apply as on a physical instrument.

## 2. The world endpoint

Beside the twin sits `/world`: the simulation’s driver, also GraphQL.
The world is where the rehearsal happens: `placeLoad(massKg:)`,
`removeLoad`, `setEnvironment(conditions:)`, `advanceTime(seconds:)`,
`scenario(name:)`. The world answers with the ground truth (the
simulation’s own state), which is what the twin’s indication is
computed from. In a browser, both endpoints serve GraphiQL for
exploration; CORS is open for browser clients in the dev posture.

## 3. Binding the endpoint in the SMART platform

The OIML-CS SMART platform binds a twin endpoint the way it would bind
a physical SMART instrument:

1. **Choose the Recommendation.** The platform’s workflow runs on a
   Recommendation’s model (today: R 60, R 91, R 129, R 144).
2. **Enter the endpoint.** The operator gives the platform the twin
   endpoint URL. The platform validates the binding: the endpoint
   answers the expected channels for the chosen Recommendation.
3. **Drive the test.** The guided run walks the test method step by
   step: the platform instructs the operator (set the chamber, place
   the load, wait), reads the twin’s indication at each point, and
   records the readings as evidence into the test run.
4. **Judge.** The recorded evidence feeds the verdict chain exactly as
   physical readings would.

The binding is deliberately identical for a simulated and a physical
endpoint; the operator is told which they are driving.
